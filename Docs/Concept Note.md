# 1. Project Overview

The proposed State-Owned Enterprises Governance, Asset & Performance Intelligence Platform (SOE-GAIP) is envisioned as a centralized digital governance and decision-intelligence system for State-Owned Enterprises under the administrative oversight of the Ministry of Industries and Production.

The platform is intended to function as a single authoritative source of information on SOE governance, ownership, assets, finances, workforce, Boards, compliance, audit, litigation, industrial performance and privatization. It should go beyond a conventional asset declaration portal by supporting financial oversight, asset management, governance monitoring and strategic decision-making.

The proposed platform will consolidate fragmented information currently maintained across SOEs, ministries, financial statements, property records, spreadsheets, audit reports and administrative files into a structured, governed and traceable digital environment.

Different categories of government users will access information according to their responsibilities while SOEs will submit, validate, certify and update their respective data through controlled reporting workflows.

| Core Proposition
SOE-GAIP is not intended to digitize isolated forms. It is intended to create a governed information chain in which official SOE data can be validated, evidenced, certified, reviewed, historically preserved and converted into executive intelligence. |
| --- |

# 2. Background and Rationale

State-Owned Enterprises manage significant public assets, employ substantial workforces and contribute to industrial production, public service delivery and national economic activity. Their performance can also create direct and indirect fiscal exposure for Government through investment, borrowing, subsidies, grants, guarantees and contingent liabilities.

Effective oversight therefore requires timely and standardized information across several dimensions:

organizational and ownership structure

financial performance

government investment and fiscal support

land, buildings, machinery and other assets

Board governance and executive management

workforce and sanctioned positions

borrowing and repayment obligations

procurement, audit and litigation

statutory compliance

industrial production and capacity utilization

privatization and restructuring

At present, these information areas may exist in different formats and at different levels of maturity across SOEs. Some information may exist in spreadsheets, some in ERP or HR systems, some in annual reports and some only in supporting documents or administrative records.

| The Strategic Question
The challenge is not simply digitization. The challenge is to create a standardized system through which Government can reliably know what each SOE owns, how it performs, what fiscal exposure Government carries, which obligations are overdue, which risks are emerging and where intervention is required. |
| --- |

# 3. Problem Statement

## 3.1 Fragmented Information

SOE information is naturally distributed across financial statements, HR records, property registers, Board records, audit reports, legal files, procurement records and administrative correspondence. Without a common platform, obtaining a complete view of an enterprise requires collecting and reconciling information from multiple sources.

## 3.2 Absence of Standardized Data

Different SOEs may maintain similar information using different classifications, formats or reporting structures. This restricts meaningful cross-SOE comparison.

## 3.3 Limited Historical Visibility

Conventional reporting can provide a current snapshot without preserving structured historical versions of individual assets, financial indicators, Board composition, compliance records and other governance information.

## 3.4 Reactive Oversight

Government oversight can become dependent on periodic reports and manual follow-up. Board expiry, overdue loan repayment, deteriorating financial performance, unresolved audit paras or missing statutory submissions may therefore be identified later than desirable.

## 3.5 Limited Decision Intelligence

A large volume of administrative information does not automatically translate into actionable intelligence. Senior leadership requires summarized, comparable and risk-oriented information rather than raw records.

# 4. Project Goal

| Goal
To establish a centralized, secure and evidence-based digital platform that enables the Government to maintain authoritative information on State-Owned Enterprises and convert that information into actionable governance, financial, asset and performance intelligence. |
| --- |

# 5. Project Objectives

Establish a standardized master registry of SOEs and their ownership structures.

Digitize and maintain comprehensive information on public assets including land, buildings, machinery, vehicles and other major asset categories.

Standardize financial, operational, governance, compliance and organizational information across participating SOEs.

Introduce structured reporting, certification, review and approval workflows.

Maintain complete historical records and audit trails of important changes.

Improve oversight of Boards, executives, workforce, audit matters, litigation and statutory compliance.

Provide decision-oriented dashboards for different levels of government leadership.

Enable cross-SOE comparison and performance benchmarking.

Introduce early-warning mechanisms for financial, governance, compliance, asset and operational risks.

Establish the digital foundation for future interoperability with external government systems.

Their primary role will be to maintain enterprise information, submit reporting data, upload supporting evidence and respond to Ministry queries.

## 6.2 MoIP Oversight Users

Relevant Wings, Section Officers, analysts and designated reviewers will focus on review, validation, clarification, monitoring and escalation.

## 6.3 Secretary-Level Users

The Secretary requires an operational governance view focused on delayed submissions, compliance gaps, outstanding approvals, financial concerns, Board vacancies, loan repayments, audit observations and pending actions.

## 6.4 Ministerial Users

The Minister requires a concise strategic view of the SOE portfolio, government investment, aggregate assets, liabilities and debt, profitability, subsidies and grants, major governance gaps, significant litigation, audit observations, privatization and underutilized assets.

## 6.5 Strategic Government Users

Subject to formal access approval, strategic users may require national-level views of government assets, return on capital, fiscal burden, potential privatization proceeds, land availability, employment, industrial output, exports and contingent liabilities.

# 18. Proposed Data Input Model

The initial system should operate independently of external government APIs. Data capture should follow a hybrid model so that low-volume governance information, high-volume operational data and supporting evidence can be handled appropriately.

| Input Channel | Best Suited For | Initial Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manual Structured Forms | Company profile, Board records, litigation, compliance, ownership and privatization stages | Primary input for judgement-based and low-volume records |
| Standard Excel / CSV Upload | Employees, assets, machinery, vehicles, procurement, loans and audit observations | Primary input for repetitive high-volume datasets |
| Document Upload | Audited accounts, property documents, notifications, court orders, valuation and audit reports | Evidence and supporting records |
| Baseline Data Migration | Existing SOE records at initial onboarding | One-time transition into the standardized platform |

## 18.1 Baseline Migration Process

| Data Collection | Mapping | Cleaning | Import | Validation | SOE Verification | Baseline Approval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

The system should support future exception-based updating so SOEs can carry forward valid information and update only additions, disposals, changes or corrections instead of repeatedly recreating full datasets.

# 19. Proposed End-to-End Workflow

| Step | Stage | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | SOE Registration | Each enterprise receives a unique system identity. |
| 2 | User & Responsibility Assignment | Users are assigned according to function and organizational responsibility. |
| 3 | Reporting Period Activation | The Ministry opens monthly, quarterly, annual or special reporting cycles. |
| 4 | Data Preparation | SOE departments prepare assigned information through forms, uploads and documents. |
| 5 | Validation | System-based checks assess completeness, formatting, consistency and unusual changes. |
| 6 | Evidence Attachment | Required supporting documents are linked to relevant records. |
| 7 | Internal SOE Review | The focal person verifies completeness across functions. |
| 8 | CEO / CFO Certification | Designated senior officials formally certify the submission where applicable. |
| 9 | MoIP Review | The relevant Ministry Wing reviews submitted information. |
| 10 | Clarification / Return | Specific records may be returned for clarification or correction. |
| 11 | Approval | Validated submissions are formally approved. |
| 12 | Historical Locking | Approved records are preserved as historical versions. |
| 13 | Intelligence Processing | Approved data feeds dashboards, KPIs, risk indicators and analytical models. |
| 14 | Continuous Monitoring | The platform generates alerts and tasks for deadlines, obligations and significant risk events. |

| Governance Principle
Executive dashboards should consume governed data. A record should not become an authoritative executive metric merely because a user entered or uploaded it. |
| --- |

# 22. High-Level Technical Architecture

The recommended initial architecture is a modular enterprise platform rather than a collection of disconnected applications. External systems are not required for the initial product to function.

| Layer | Primary Components |
| --- | --- |
| Presentation Layer | SOE Portal, MoIP Oversight Portal and Executive Intelligence Portal |
| Application Layer | Business modules, workflow, certification, task management, validation and reporting |
| Data Layer | Relational database, geospatial database, document storage, historical records and audit trail |
| Intelligence Layer | KPIs, dashboards, risk indicators, GIS analytics, scorecards, early warning and reporting |

| Architecture Principle
Centralized authoritative data, decentralized departmental input, controlled governance workflows and a separate intelligence layer. |
| --- |

# 23. External Government Integrations - Future Phase

External API integrations should be treated as a separate post-development phase. The initial platform must operate fully through structured manual entry, standardized bulk upload, document submission and controlled baseline migration.

Potential future interoperability may include relevant systems maintained by:

SECP

FBR

AGPR

Auditor General

PPRA

Privatization Commission

Pakistan Bureau of Statistics

provincial land record authorities

other authorized government institutions

The core data model should nevertheless remain integration-ready by preserving stable internal identifiers and external reference fields so future integrations can be added without redesigning the authoritative SOE records.

# 24. Information Security and Governance

The system will contain sensitive institutional, financial, legal and personal data. Security and information governance must therefore be designed into the platform from the outset.

role-based access control

organization-level access restrictions

field-level restrictions for sensitive information

multi-factor authentication

encryption in transit and at rest

secure document storage

immutable audit logs

session controls

backup and disaster recovery

secure software development practices

activity monitoring

controlled administrative access

Detailed security requirements should be finalized in consultation with relevant Government technology and cybersecurity authorities before production deployment.

# 25. Proposed Implementation Approach

| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 1 | Requirements & Data Standardization | Validate SOE universe, finalize roles, conduct data discovery, define data dictionary, reporting frequencies, validation rules, KPIs and approval hierarchy. |

# 29. Strategic Value and Long-Term Vision

The principal value of SOE-GAIP is not the digitization of forms. Its strategic value is the creation of a governed information chain in which enterprise information becomes authoritative, traceable and usable for decision-making.

| SOE Data | Validation | Evidence | Certification | MoIP Review | Authoritative Record | Risk & Intelligence | Executive Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

This approach transforms fragmented administrative information into structured public-sector intelligence and creates the foundation for industrial asset mapping, early warning, standardized performance scorecards and decision-support analysis.

| Long-Term Vision
Every State-Owned Enterprise, every material public asset, every major governance obligation and every significant performance risk available through one authoritative decision-intelligence platform. |
| --- |

If implemented in phases and supported by strong data governance, SOE-GAIP can evolve from a Ministry oversight portal into a national institutional capability for SOE governance, asset intelligence, fiscal-risk monitoring and strategic public-sector decision-making.
